# Shortcut

*A short game about taking the easy way, and finding the way back.*

This is the story of the game, told the way it plays. It takes about an hour to an hour and a half, and you can stop and pick it up again whenever you like.

---

## The Start Line

It's dusk. A small figure stands at the start of a long, empty track. Before anything else happens, a note appears:

> "Euan, I made something for you. It takes a while. Please play it to the end."

You take your first steps forward.

## Training Grounds

The track leads into a quiet practice yard lit by a single lantern. The one holding it is the Mentor: patient, calm, always a step ahead, lighting the way.

Here you learn the basics, how to jump, when to wait, how to keep going when something takes a few tries. At the end of each little room is a stone with a lesson carved into it:

> *Slow is smooth. Smooth is fast.*
> *The work is the point.*
> *Nobody sees practice. It still counts.*

There are six of them. By the time you've found them all, you're ready for the real thing.

## The Qualifier

The competition begins. Three courses, each harder than the last: platforms that drift, wind that pushes you back, a bridge that only holds for a few seconds. It's fair, but it's tiring. A cheerful rival keeps overtaking you.

Then you reach a wall that seems just a little too high to climb. You try. The music goes quiet.

## The Shortcut

Right beside the wall, a door starts to glow: **SHORTCUT →**

You go through it. It's the only way the story goes on.

And at first it works. You're suddenly ahead. But more shortcut doors keep appearing, and every one of them goes wrong in its own ridiculous way. Gravity flips and you're walking on the ceiling. The path loops you straight back to where you started. Your jump button turns into "sit down". A shiny pile of free coins turns out to be painted on the floor. And at some point, without you noticing, your shadow quietly stops following you.

Bit by bit you learn that every level can be finished without the shortcuts, and that it's actually easier that way. Small lines drift past:

> "It looked faster."
> "It wasn't."
> "Cheating always backfires. Usually on you. Sometimes on people you care about."

Each mistake leaves behind a little card for your collection.

## The Win That Wasn't

You cross the finish line first.

The podium is made of cardboard. The trophy is hollow. The cheering crowd is a painted backdrop, and it slowly tips over. The Mentor's lantern sits on the bottom step of the podium, but the Mentor isn't there.

You walk through the empty stadium alone.

> "I won."
> "It didn't feel like anything."
> "You weren't there to see it. I'm glad you weren't."

## Chasing My Shadow

Then you spot it: your shadow, the one you left behind, and it runs.

You chase it back through everything you've already been through, in reverse. This time the shortcut doors are boarded up, so you have to do it properly. It starts out as a silly chase, but it doesn't stay that way. Every so often the shadow stops and holds up a sign:

> "You cheated."
> "He trusted you."

To catch it, you have to stop running from what the signs say. When you finally do, you and your shadow become one again.

## The Long Way Back

Night. You pick up the Mentor's lantern and start the long walk back along the real track. No shortcuts, no hurry.

Along the way you find scattered words. Three times you stop to rest and try to put them together into an apology. Some of the words are excuses ("but…", "everyone does it", "it wasn't a big deal"), and when you try to use them they crumble to dust. Only the honest words fit.

As you walk, the words come together:

> "You believed in me."
> "I cheated, and that let you down."
> "It was wrong. There's no excuse for it."
> "I know it hurt you."

## The Apology

At the end of the track, the Mentor is waiting.

You hand back the lantern. Then, one line at a time:

> "Euan, I'm sorry. I understand that I hurt you, and that what I did was bad. I learned that cheating always backfires, and I want to earn things the honest way from now on."

For a moment, nothing else happens. That's on purpose.

## The Honest Run

Dawn. You're back at the Qualifier, and this time the Mentor walks beside you.

The wall is still there, just as high as before. But now you know how to climb it, using everything the lesson stones taught you at the very beginning. You reach the finish line the honest way.

There's no podium and no score this time. Just:

> "Thank you for playing."

## A few extra things to find

- All six lesson stones and all ten backfire cards, kept in a little gallery.
- Hidden notes tucked into quiet corners of each chapter.
- After the ending, an open practice yard where you can keep running for fun. Every shortcut door in it is locked.
